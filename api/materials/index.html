    Gas, adsorbent and column¶

    swingpy.gas ¶

    Properties of the gas mixture that flows through the column.

    The gas is an ideal mixture of any number of components. Its transport properties are taken as constant, which is the usual assumption of the detailed adsorption models this package follows. The adiabatic constant, which only enters the energy of compressors and vacuum pumps, may depend on composition.

    GAS_CONSTANT = 8.314 module-attribute ¶

    Universal gas constant in J/(mol K).

    MOLAR_MASSES = {'CO2': 0.04401, 'N2': 0.028014, 'O2': 0.031999, 'Ar': 0.039948, 'CH4': 0.016043, 'CO': 0.02801} module-attribute ¶

    Molar masses in kg/mol of common gases, used when none are given.

    gamma(mole_fractions) ¶

    Adiabatic constant for a given composition.

    LinearCO2AdiabaticConstant dataclass ¶

    Adiabatic constant of CO2/N2 mixtures as a linear function of the CO2 mole fraction, gamma = 1.395 - 0.105 y_CO2, a linear fit of NIST reference data between pure N2 and pure CO2.

    Pass an instance as GasMixture.adiabatic_constant. It matters for the vacuum pumps that handle CO2-rich gas: compared with a constant gamma = 1.4, it lowers the evacuation work by about 10 % at 0.02 to 0.03 bar.

    Position of CO2 in GasMixture.components.

    swingpy.adsorbent ¶

    Adsorbent particles and their mass transfer kinetics.

    MacroporeLDF dataclass ¶

    Linear driving force rate controlled by molecular diffusion in macropores.

    The rate constant of component i is

    k_i = (c_i / q_i*) 15 eps_p D_p / r_p^2,   with D_p = D_m / tortuosity,

    where q_i* is expressed per cubic metre of adsorbent so that c_i / q_i* is dimensionless (q_i* in mol/kg times the particle density), the Glueckauf approximation for diffusion in a spherical particle.

    A component that the solid barely holds (q_i* / c_i close to zero, for example nitrogen displaced by water) gets an enormous rate constant, which only means that it is at equilibrium; such rates are capped at max_rate (1/s), far above any rate that matters, so that the equations do not become needlessly stiff.

    rate_constants(slopes, adsorbent, gas, pressure=None, temperature=None) ¶

    LDF rate constants in 1/s, with the shape of slopes (m3/kg).

    ConstantLDF dataclass ¶

    Linear driving force with fixed rate constants.

    Attributes:

    Name Type Description
    rates Sequence[float]

    One rate constant per gas component, 1/s.

    PressureScaledLDF dataclass ¶

    Linear driving force with rate constants inversely proportional to pressure,

    k_i = rates_i * reference_pressure / P,

    the behaviour of uptake controlled by molecular diffusion in the macropores (the molecular diffusivity scales as 1/P). Uptake is then much faster at low pressure (blowdown, purge) than at high pressure (feed).

    Attributes:

    Name Type Description
    rates Sequence[float]

    Rate constants at the reference pressure, 1/s, one per component.
    reference_pressure float

    bar.

    SeriesLDF dataclass ¶

    Linear driving force for a bidisperse particle, with the film, macropore and micropore resistances added in series (Glueckauf; Ruthven, Principles of Adsorption and Adsorption Processes, 1984, Eq. 8.53 and 8.54):

    1 / k_i = K_i / (a_p k_f) + K_i R_p^2 / (Omega_m eps_p D_p,i) + r_c^2 / (Omega_c D_c,i),
K_i = rho_p q_i* / c_i,   D_c,i / r_c^2 = (D_c0 / r_c^2)_i exp(-E_a,i / RT),

    where K_i is the dimensionless slope of the isotherm per volume of particle (the secant q*/c, as in MacroporeLDF), a_p the external area per volume of particle (3 / R_p for spheres, 2 / R_p for cylinders) and Omega the LDF factors (15 for spheres, 8 for cylinders). This is a single LDF that stands in for a bi-LDF model, where the macropore gas and the micropore loading are separate states; it is exact for a linear isotherm at long times and an approximation otherwise, so say so when you use it.

    Attributes:

    Name Type Description
    micropore_rates Sequence[float]

    (D_c0 / r_c^2)_i, 1/s, one per component (inf for no micropore resistance).
    activation_energies Sequence[float]

    E_a,i, J/mol (zero for a constant D_c/r_c^2).
    macropore_diffusivities Sequence[float]

    D_p,i, m2/s (inf for none).
    film_coefficient float

    k_f, m/s (inf for no film resistance).
    micropore_factor float

    Omega_c, -.
    macropore_factor float

    Omega_m, -.
    area_factor float

    a_p R_p, - (3 for spheres, 2 for cylinders).
    max_rate float

    Cap on the rate constants, 1/s (see MacroporeLDF).

    LayeredBed ¶

    A bed of several adsorbent layers along the column, for example a guard layer of alumina or activated carbon at the feed end and a zeolite above it, as in most industrial PSA units.

    Pass it to ColumnModel in place of an Adsorbent. Every layer keeps its own isotherm, kinetics, density, heat capacity, heats of adsorption, particle size and, optionally, bed voidage, applied to the cells it fills; the pressure drop and axial dispersion of every face follow from the particle size and voidage on both sides of it.

    Parameters:

    Name Type Description Default
    layers

    (Adsorbent, length fraction) or (Adsorbent, length fraction, bed voidage) tuples from the feed end to the product end; the fractions must add up to 1. A layer without a voidage takes that of the column.

    		 required
    name

    Display name.

    		 None

    layer_cells(n_cells) ¶

    Cell indices of every layer (each cell belongs to the layer that holds its centre).

    cell_view(n_cells) ¶

    An Adsorbent-like object whose properties are arrays over the cells.

    ParticleDiffusion dataclass ¶

    Fickian diffusion inside spherical particles (or crystals), resolved in concentric shells, instead of a linear driving force:

    dq/dt = D / r^2 d/dr (r^2 dq/dr),   q(r_p) = q*(c, T) at the surface.

    This is the model of uptake controlled by diffusion in the micropores of a crystal or a carbon molecular sieve, where it matters for kinetic separations; for a single particle at constant surface loading it reproduces the exact solution of Crank (The Mathematics of Diffusion, 1975, Eq. 6.20), to which a linear driving force with k = 15 D / r_p^2 is the Glueckauf approximation.

    The particle is divided into shells shells, thinner towards the surface, and solved by finite volumes; the uptake of a cell is the flux through the particle surface, so mass is conserved exactly.

    Attributes:

    Name Type Description
    rates Sequence[float]

    Diffusional time constants D_i / r_p^2, 1/s, one per component.
    shells int

    Number of shells (12 by default).

    swingpy.column ¶

    Geometry and thermal properties of a packed adsorption column.

    Column dataclass ¶

    A cylindrical packed bed with a steel wall.

    Attributes:

    Name Type Description
    length float

    Bed length, m.
    inner_radius float

    m.
    outer_radius float

    m. Only used by the wall energy balance.
    bed_voidage float

    Interparticle voidage, -.
    pressure_drop str

    "darcy" (viscous term only) or "ergun" (viscous and inertial terms).
    axial_thermal_conductivity float

    Effective axial conductivity of the bed, W/(m K).
    inner_heat_transfer float

    Bed to wall coefficient, W/(m2 K). Zero for an adiabatic column.
    outer_heat_transfer float

    Wall to ambient coefficient, W/(m2 K).
    wall_density float

    kg/m3.
    wall_heat_capacity float

    J/(kg K).
    wall_thermal_conductivity float

    W/(m K).
    include_pore_gas bool

    Count the gas in the pores of the particles as gas holdup: the gas balance then uses the total voidage eps_t = eps_b + (1 - eps_b) eps_p (eps_p is the particle voidage of the adsorbent), while the flow through the bed and the solid fraction use the bed voidage eps_b. Off by default, as in models that lump the pore gas into the solid; it matters most for weakly adsorbed gases such as nitrogen, whose front it slows down.
    dead_volume_feed, dead_volume_product

    Well-mixed gas volume at each end of the bed (pipes, distributors, valve bodies), m3. It holds gas but no adsorbent, and slows the pressure and composition changes at that end; it is often what makes small laboratory columns behave differently from the model of the bed alone.
    ambient_temperature float

    Temperature outside the wall, K.

    cross_section property ¶

    Inner cross-sectional area, m2.

    adsorbent_volume property ¶

    Volume of the solid particles, (1 - eps) A L, m3.
