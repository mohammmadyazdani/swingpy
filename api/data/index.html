    Gas properties, humidity and equation of state¶

    swingpy.data ¶

    Pure-component gas properties and mixture property estimates.

    A bank of pure-component properties (molar mass, ideal-gas heat capacity, viscosity, Fuller diffusion volume, critical constants and acentric factor, each kind with its source, gas_sources()), and estimates for mixtures: Fuller molecular diffusivity and Wilke viscosity. Your own components can be added with register_gas.

    from swingpy import data

data.list_gases()
data.gas_properties("CO2")
gas = data.gas(["CO2", "N2"], {"CO2": 0.15, "N2": 0.85}, temperature=298.15, pressure=1.0)

    gas_properties(name) ¶

    Molar mass (kg/mol), heat capacity (J/(mol K)), viscosity (Pa s) and Fuller diffusion volume of a gas.

    wilke_viscosity(components, mole_fractions, temperature=298.15) ¶

    Viscosity of a gas mixture by Wilke's mixing rule, Pa s (pure viscosities scaled as T^0.7).

    gas(components, composition=None, temperature=298.15, pressure=1.0, **overrides) ¶

    A GasMixture with properties evaluated for a representative composition.

    The heat capacity is the mole-fraction average, the viscosity follows Wilke's mixing rule, and the molecular diffusivity is the Fuller binary diffusivity of the first two components (the key pair) at the given temperature and pressure. These are constants of the model, so evaluate them at the feed conditions.

    swingpy.humidity ¶

    Water vapour: saturation pressure, relative humidity and dew point.

    Flue gases and air carry water, which zeolites take up far more strongly than CO2. These helpers turn a relative humidity into a mole fraction for the feed composition, and back.

    The saturation pressure follows the Buck equation over liquid water, accurate to about 0.05 % between 0 and 100 C.

    saturation_pressure(temperature) ¶

    Saturation pressure of water over a flat liquid surface, bar, for a temperature in K (Buck equation).

    swingpy.eos ¶

    Equations of state of the gas phase.

    from swingpy.eos import PengRobinson

eos = PengRobinson(["CO2", "H2"])                     # constants of the gas data bank
eos = PengRobinson(["CO2", "H2"], constants=[(304.13, 73.77, 0.2239), (33.15, 12.96, -0.219)])
Z = eos.compressibility(T, P, y)        # T in K, P in bar; y one composition or (n_components, n_points)
phi = eos.fugacity_coefficients(T, P, y)

    IdealGas gives Z = 1 and fugacity coefficients of one. PengRobinson is the cubic equation of Peng and Robinson, Ind. Eng. Chem. Fundam. 15 (1976) 59, https://doi.org/10.1021/i160057a011, with the van der Waals one-fluid mixing rules

    a = sum_i sum_j y_i y_j (1 - k_ij) sqrt(a_i a_j),   b = sum_i y_i b_i,
a_i = Omega_a R^2 Tc_i^2 / pc_i [1 + kappa_i (1 - sqrt(T / Tc_i))]^2,   b_i = Omega_b R Tc_i / pc_i,
kappa_i = 0.37464 + 1.54226 w_i - 0.26992 w_i^2   (``kappa="1978"``: the form of
    Robinson and Peng (1978) for w > 0.491)
Z^3 - (1 - B) Z^2 + (A - 3 B^2 - 2 B) Z - (A B - B^2 - B^3) = 0,
    A = a p / (R T)^2, B = b p / (R T); the gas takes the largest root

    Omega_a = 0.457235529 and Omega_b = 0.0777960739 are the exact values that follow from the conditions at the critical point; the article rounds them to 0.45724 and 0.07780 (which changes Z by about 3e-6 at 35 bar).

    Critical constants and acentric factors come from the gas data (data.gas_properties) or are given (constants). Binary interaction parameters are zero unless given; they are mixture specific and should come from a fit to data.

    The column model uses the ideal gas law; this equation of state checks how far the gas is from ideal (analysis.compressibility_range, the gas.compressibility_check section of a specification).

    IdealGas ¶

    The ideal gas: Z = 1, fugacity coefficients 1, no residual enthalpy.
