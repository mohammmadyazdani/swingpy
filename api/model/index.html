    Column model¶

    swingpy.model ¶

    Finite volume model of a nonisothermal, nonisobaric adsorption column.

    The column is described by a one-dimensional, axially dispersed plug flow model of an ideal gas mixture with any number of components, linear driving force uptake, a bed energy balance with heat of adsorption and heat loss to the wall, a wall energy balance, and Darcy or Ergun pressure drop. The equations are written in dimensionless, conservative form and solved on N equal cells.

    Scaling. Pressure is divided by a reference pressure P0, temperature by T0, velocity by v0, length by the column length L, time by L / v0 and loadings by a reference loading q_s0. With L = 1 m and v0 = 1 m/s the dimensionless time equals time in seconds.

    State vector. For n components the state holds, in this order:

    y          n - 1 independent mole fractions, N values each
P          pressure, N values
x          n loadings, N values each
T          bed temperature, N values
T_wall     wall temperature, N values
bookkeeping  running integrals at both ends: n component fluxes,
             the enthalpy flux and the machine work, per end

    The bookkeeping integrals make purity, recovery, energy and balance errors as accurate as the time integration itself.

    qs0_volumetric = reference_loading * adsorbent.density instance-attribute ¶

    Bed voidage and particle size of every cell and face. They are uniform except in a layered bed. The velocity v is the superficial velocity divided by the column voidage eps (eps_ref), so the end faces convert flows as in a uniform bed; a cell of voidage eps_j accumulates gas in proportion to eps_j, so its flux divergences are scaled by eps_ref/eps_j.

    peclet = reference_velocity * column.length / dispersion instance-attribute ¶

    Without axial conduction the heat Peclet number is infinite: an inlet face takes the inlet temperature.

    heat_peclet = eps * reference_velocity * column.length * self.c0 * gas.heat_capacity / column.axial_thermal_conductivity if column.axial_thermal_conductivity > 0.0 else np.inf instance-attribute ¶

    Coupling of the solid uptake to the gas balances.

    pi_3 = 2.0 * column.outer_radius * column.outer_heat_transfer / wall_ring * self.time_scale / wall_capacity instance-attribute ¶

    Ratio of axial conduction to gas enthalpy convection at the ends.

    danckwerts(cell, y_in, t_in, inflow_speed, end=None) ¶

    Face composition and temperature of an inlet (Danckwerts conditions): f = (f_cell + f_in a) / (1 + a) with a = v Pe dZ / 2. With no inflow this reduces to the zero gradient condition.

    molar_inflow_face(molar_flow, y_full, temperature_k, cell, end) ¶

    Face state of an inlet with an imposed molar flow (mol/s), used by recorded and tank streams. The velocity is v = G T / P with G the dimensionless molar flux, and the face pressure must drive that velocity through half a cell. For Darcy's law the two conditions give a quadratic in the face pressure, solved exactly; the Ergun term is added by a few fixed-point iterations.

    rhs(tau, state, step) ¶

    Time derivative of the state vector during step.

    simulate(step, state, n_output=50, rtol=1e-05, atol=1e-07, max_evaluations=40000, fallback=True) ¶

    Integrate one step from its start to step.duration, or until its stop condition is met.

    The bookkeeping integrals are reset at the start, so the returned flows and work belong to this step only. A step whose stop condition already holds is skipped and returns a zero-length result.

    The stiff BDF solver is used first. The upwind side of each face follows the local velocity, so the right hand side is not smooth where the velocity changes sign; in a step whose gas is nearly at rest over part of the column (a blowdown or evacuation of a short, very permeable bed) Newton's method then keeps failing and BDF can take hundreds of thousands of evaluations. After max_evaluations right hand side evaluations the step is restarted with LSODA, which switches between non-stiff and stiff methods and handles this far better (a 2 s blowdown of a 1 cm direct air capture bed: 81 000 evaluations with BDF, 11 500 with LSODA). max_evaluations=None keeps BDF however long it takes. With fallback=False the step raises StepTooStiff instead of switching to LSODA (used to discard an extrapolated cycle start that turns out to be hard to integrate).

    wall_heat(result, positive_only=False) ¶

    Heat supplied to the column from its surroundings through the wall during a step, J (negative when the column loses heat), from the outer heat transfer coefficient and the wall temperatures over time. In a temperature swing cycle the surroundings are a heating or cooling jacket, set per step with Step(ambient_temperature=...).

    With positive_only=True only the heat flowing into the column counts, the integral of max(0, Q_in) over time: heat removed by the cooling fluid is not credited back.

    heat_of_adsorption_released(initial_state, final_state) ¶

    end_history(result, end='product') ¶

    Velocity (m/s), pressure (bar), mole fractions and temperature (K) at one end face over the output times of a step, for plots.

    outflow_record(result, end) ¶

    Record of the gas leaving one end during a step (molar flow in mol/s, composition, temperature), to be replayed by a later step.

    profile_change(previous_state, state) ¶

    Largest change of any profile (y, P, x, T, T_wall) between two states, in dimensionless units, where every profile is of order one: 1e-3 means about 0.3 K at T0 = 300 K, or 0.1 % of the reference pressure. Used as a cyclic steady state test on the column itself.

    swingpy.schemes ¶

    Face reconstruction schemes of the finite volume method.

    A finite volume needs the value of each transported quantity at the faces between cells. The schemes below compute it from the cell averages on the upwind side: first order upwind, two flux-limited TVD schemes and WENO.
